What Babuwatch does

One register of what the courts decided about public servants

Record

We record convictions and adverse court findings against public servants of every government in India — Union, state and local — and cite the court and the source for each one.

Attribute

Every finding belongs to the court that made it. We add no findings of our own, and we say plainly how far each record has been verified.

Follow up

Trial convictions can be appealed. Each record shows its appeal status as known from the sources, and convictions later set aside leave the counts.

Protect

Names are withheld unless a two-key naming gate clears them. Complainants, victims, witnesses and minors are never named.

One register covers every public servant — police officers and civil servants of departments, municipal bodies and other government offices alike. Every record passes the same verification and naming rules, whatever the service.

Lawful routes

There are lawful routes, and free legal aid is a right for everyone who qualifies. Corruption by a public servant is reported to the authorities — your state’s Anti-Corruption Bureau or Vigilance department, the Lokayukta where one exists, the Central Bureau of Investigation for Union government employees, or the Central Vigilance Commission — and harm or unlawful detention can go to the courts and the human rights commissions. Take the sources on any record to a lawyer and keep copies of every complaint. Babuwatch itself is a record, not a complaints desk: contacting us does not notify any authority or begin any case.

Who gets free legal aid

Under Section 12 of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, free legal services are available to anyone who has to file or defend a case and is: a woman or a child; a member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe; a person with a disability; a victim of trafficking or forced labour; a victim of a mass disaster, ethnic or caste violence, flood, drought, earthquake or industrial disaster; an industrial workman; a person in custody; or a person whose annual income is below ₹3 lakh, or the higher limit set by their state. Apply in writing or in person at the District Legal Services Authority or Taluka Legal Services Committee, or call the national legal-aid helpline 15100. Read NALSA’s eligibility guidance, and check your State Legal Services Authority for its own income limit.
