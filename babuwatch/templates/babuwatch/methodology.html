How we work

Methodology & disclaimer

Babuwatch publishes only what a court decided, attributed to that court, and says exactly how far each record has been checked.

Public statement

Babuwatch documents court-adjudicated findings against public servants in India, drawn from court judgments and from the official conviction records that anti-corruption and vigilance agencies publish. Every finding is the court's, not ours. Inclusion records what a court decided on the date shown; it does not establish anything beyond that finding, and a trial-court conviction may since have been appealed. Records are corrected or withdrawn as facts develop. We withhold names by default and never publish the identities of complainants, victims, witnesses or minors.

One method, several registers

One register, one method

Babuwatch lists every published record in one register — police officers and civil servants alike. Every record shows the verification level it was published at, and the appendix below sets out what each level means and how each source programme’s rules apply. Police records began life as Copwatch India, and that provenance is kept in their sources. Further registers (for example, for a single service) will follow the same method.

Official sources only. A record is published only when one of its sources is the court order itself or an official agency record (a court or government website, a judgment repository, or an archived copy of one). A record that rests on news reports alone is held back until an official copy is found, and its page says so. High Court and Supreme Court records publish at V2 and above.

Who is covered. Babuwatch covers the core civil services first: the All India Services (IAS, IPS and the Indian Forest Service), the Indian Foreign Service and central services such as the IRS, and state and central government employees from Group A (formerly Class I) to Group C (Class III). Employees of public-sector enterprises, government banks among them, will be added after that.
