Civil-servant records

Civil-servant convictions

These rules govern records of civil servants. Police records follow Copwatch India's methodology; the verification levels, the naming gate and the treatment of appeals are the same in both.

Inclusion rules

Who. An employee of a Union, state or local government department, office or body — for example a revenue official, clerk, engineer, registrar, or municipal or panchayat employee — convicted for conduct in that office. Elected representatives, employees of public-sector banks and companies, and private persons (co-accused, middlemen) are not included in this register.

What. A criminal conviction entered by a trial court — a Special Judge under the Prevention of Corruption Act, a CBI court, a Lokayukta court or a Sessions court — recorded either in the judgment itself or in an official conviction list, press release or annual report of the agency that prosecuted the case (a Lokayukta, Anti-Corruption Bureau, Vigilance department, the Directorate of Vigilance and Anti-Corruption, or the CBI). Acquittals, discharges, pending trials and departmental action alone are not included.

Verification

A civil-servant record publishes only at V2: the official source was retrieved (an archived copy where the agency has since removed the page), and the official's post, the court, the conviction date or case number, and the conviction itself were matched in the same passage of that source. A record where any of these could not be matched is withheld as V1; a record where the source reports an acquittal for that person is rejected. V2 means the record is accurately attributed to an official source; it does not mean Babuwatch has independently corroborated the underlying facts (that is V3).

Appeals

Agency conviction lists rarely report what happened on appeal. “No appeal known” means only that the cited source does not mention an appeal; it is not a statement that none was filed. Trial convictions are appealable and some are set aside; a conviction known to have been set aside leaves the counts. If you know a conviction was overturned, tell us and we will correct the record.

Naming

Officials are shown by post and department. A name appears only after the two-key naming gate used across Babuwatch: the source must show that person convicted (first key), an independent check must confirm it (second key), a subsequent-history check must find no later reversal, and a qualified lawyer must review the record. No civil-servant name has yet cleared this gate, so none is shown. Complainants and other private persons are never named.

Categories

Each record carries one category: Bribery & extortion (demanding or accepting a bribe, Prevention of Corruption Act section 7 and related provisions); Disproportionate assets (assets beyond known income, section 13(1)(e), section 13(1)(b) after the 2018 amendment); Fraud & misappropriation (criminal breach of trust, cheating, forgery, misappropriation of public money); Violence & unlawful detention (almost entirely police records); and Other misconduct.

Duplicates

The same conviction often appears in several lists (an annual report and a press release, or two years of reports). These are merged into one record, keeping the best-evidenced source.

Limitations

Coverage reflects which agencies publish conviction records and how far back their archives reach. Several states publish none, so absence from Babuwatch is not evidence of absence. These records are not a representative sample of corruption or of prosecutions; counts show what was found and verified, not how common misconduct is. Dates are as precise as the source allows.

Corrections

If a record is wrong, incomplete or out of date, write to us with the record's link and the source that shows the correction. A record we cannot stand behind is corrected or withdrawn, and the change is logged.
