About

What Babuwatch is

A public register of what India's courts decided about the people who exercise state power on the public's behalf.

Public servants hold power that ordinary citizens do not: to grant or withhold a licence, register a document, measure a plot of land, sanction a payment, arrest, or detain. When a court finds that power was abused, that finding is a public fact. It is usually scattered across court websites, agency annual reports and press releases, and it is soon forgotten. Babuwatch gathers it in one place.

Babuwatch is a family of registers under one method. Copwatch India records court findings against police officers. Babuwatch itself records convictions of civil servants — the clerks, revenue officials, engineers, registrars and officers of every government department and local body — and lists every record from every register together.

What we hold ourselves to

  1. Only what a court decided. Every record rests on a court judgment or an official record of the conviction, and every finding is attributed to the court that made it.
  2. Say how far it was checked. Every record shows its verification level and its appeal status as known from the sources.
  3. Names are earned, not assumed. Officials are shown by post and department unless the naming gate clears a name. Complainants, victims and witnesses are never named.
  4. Correct in public. A record we cannot stand behind is corrected or withdrawn, promptly and visibly.

What Babuwatch is not

It is not a complaints service, a helpline or a legal service, and it does not forward reports to any authority. It is not a measure of how common corruption is: it shows the convictions that official sources record and that we could verify. Nothing on this site is legal advice.

Babuwatch is a Data Libertarian project.
