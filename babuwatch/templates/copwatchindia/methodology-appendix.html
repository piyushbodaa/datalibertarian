Court-adjudicated records

This section covers the court-judgment dataset published on this site. It supplements the verification policy above; where the two differ, this section governs these records. Every record is a judgment, not a news allegation: findings are attributed to the deciding court, and this record does not add findings of its own.

Inclusion rules

A case is included only if its resolved verification verdict is accept: the judgment is from the Supreme Court of India or a High Court and records an adverse finding against police personnel — a criminal conviction entered or upheld, compensation for a custodial, encounter, or detention wrong, or sustained service punishment. Flagged cases (judgment text not located, single news report only) and rejected cases are excluded from the dataset and all counts. Records are continuing records: they may be updated as new information, official responses, or court findings become available.

Verification levels for court records

The same four levels apply. V0 (submitted) and V1 (provenance checked) are internal only and never published. V2 means a qualifying public source — here, the judgment — has been authenticated and accurately attributed, but the incident has not been independently corroborated; V2 publishes only with clear attribution and allegation framing. V3 means at least one material fact has been corroborated through independently verifiable material, a verified named confirmation, or a method-disclosed verification, with the corroborated fact and its limits stated accurately. A verified public court record may corroborate a material fact at V3 only when an editor records what the document establishes and how its authenticity was checked. All AI-only accepts ship as V2 · Externally reported; nothing is promoted to V3 without the full checklist below.

Tiers

Tier A — Externally reported or officially recorded: sourced from public court records and attributed to them; not independently verified by Copwatch India. We are documenting what a qualifying external source reports or records; we are not ourselves making the underlying accusation. Tier B — Copwatch-verified: records for which we have independently corroborated at least one material fact to V3 through our own verification work. A record may graduate from Tier A to Tier B; where we later corroborate an externally reported case ourselves, its tier changes and its source field records both origins.

V3 promotion checklist

Every item is required before a court record may wear V3: a recorded authenticity check (host plus cross-check, e.g. eCourts or official High Court site text against citation and paragraph numbering); the corroborated fact, the material supporting it, and its limits; privacy review; publication basis; recorded reviews; and qualified-lawyer legal review wherever an officer is named. An established outlet’s own method-disclosed verification is recorded as that outlet’s work and attributed to it; such a record remains Tier A. Where an outlet asserts facts without disclosing method, the record stays at V2.

Per-allegation positions

V0–V3 describes the record as a whole; we also state an evidence position for every allegation recorded within it, so support for one fact is not silently applied to every allegation. Reported allegation means a source makes or records the claim. Material fact supported means reliable material supports a specifically documented fact relevant to that allegation — not necessarily the full allegation, causation, intent, unlawfulness, or responsibility. We separately identify where an official finding supports or rejects an allegation, where credible material conflicts, or where the available material does not permit an assessment. A custody record may support that detention occurred without establishing that it was unlawful; a court finding about unlawful detention is not automatically applied to separate allegations of torture or the circumstances of a death.

Institutional response and follow-up

We record each action separately with its date, source, authority, independence from the accused body, legal effect, status, and any disposition. A pending action has no disposition. A concluded action has an explicit disposition, including where it was closed without findings. An appeal is a separate action linked to the decision under review; ‘Concluded’ applies only to that particular action. Acquittals, exonerations, dismissals, closure reports, orders set aside, and relief denied are retained alongside convictions, findings, discipline, and relief granted. We return to published proceedings and record dated, source-linked follow-up checks; each check targets one institutional action or one precisely stated question, records when it was performed with the exact period searched or an explicit point-in-time date, and states the method consulted. The check describes the search, not the world: ‘no new development was found within the stated search’ and ‘the search was unable to establish the current position’ are never combined. ‘Never re-checked’ is derived from the absence of a published check; a check older than 180 days is shown as stale, with its original date and finding remaining visible.

Outcome types

Each record carries one outcome type: adverse finding + compensation (court records an adverse finding and orders compensation without criminally convicting any officer); conviction upheld (appeal against conviction of police personnel failed); conviction entered by High Court (High Court convicts, typically reversing a trial acquittal); conviction entered by Supreme Court (Supreme Court convicts, typically reversing a High Court acquittal); disciplinary action upheld (court sustains service punishment after a misconduct proceeding). Each judgment outcome and each appeal renders as its own action card with status and disposition; initiation or pendency of an appeal is not a finding.

Categories

Public categories use the site vocabulary only: Public beating, Protest policing, Custodial death, Custodial torture, Other. Each record carries exactly one Primary allegation. House coding stays internal; sub-category renders as lowercase free text and is never a public filter. Protest policing applies only when the adjudicated facts concern protest policing.

How judgments were gathered

Candidate judgments were scouted from Indian Kanoon as the primary discovery index, cross-checked on general web search, with judgment text mirrored from LiveLaw, Verdictum, CourtKutchehry, LawBeat, SCC summaries, CaseMine, official High Court sites, eCourts, and commission leads. Verification agents re-checked each entry against the judgment, citation, and URL and returned accept, flag, or reject. The compiler reconciled duplicate verifications by normalised judgment URL (falling back to title match), merged same-judgment duplicates found under different URLs, and unions all source URLs. The compiler does not re-verify judgments: it reconciles, deduplicates, and formats verification output.

Naming: officers

Individual officers are named only from a concluded official action: a name may be published only where a public court, commission, or official source specifically names that officer in a concluded formal action, the source is linked to the name, and a qualified lawyer performs the legal review. A newsroom attribution alone is not sufficient for a name to enter our structured public record. Below that threshold we describe the incident, the category, and the police station or unit.

Why some officers are unnamed

Every officer name passes a deterministic two-key plus subsequent-history gate before it may appear. The first key is the judgment ledger: the officer’s role in the judgment must be convicted or held liable. The second key is independent confirmation that the judgment does name that officer for that role. The subsequent-history check then withholds the name if a later outcome set the conviction aside or acquitted the officer on appeal, or if a high-risk review flags the naming. Officers who fail any part of the gate appear as rank and unit only (for example “Sub-Inspector, Kammapuram PS”); their names appear nowhere on the public site — not in tables, search, descriptions, or data downloads. This is deliberate: a name in a permanent, structured record outlives a news cycle, and naming a specific officer is where legal liability concentrates.

Naming: victims, minors, survivors

We redact personal information to protect victims, witnesses, minors, and vulnerable persons. We do not publish faces of minors, identities of sexual offence survivors, home addresses, phone numbers, identity document details, or unnecessary family details. The editorial choices of the original source do not set our standard: even where a judgment names a minor or a survivor, this site redacts to role nouns (“a 15-year-old boy”, “the complainant”, “a woman detainee”). Case titles that would themselves identify a protected person use a redacted display title. Privacy review is mandatory at every verification status. We withhold a link where it could identify or endanger a victim, witness, minor, or other vulnerable person, even if the original page remains public.

Social media and restricted material

Social media is a lead, not verification: a public post is not published as a verified incident merely because it is publicly accessible. Material visible only to restricted audiences, from private accounts, or via leak is not treated as a public source. A person’s own public post is not blanket consent for us to reproduce it, and a request not to publish blocks publication while it is reviewed.

Review policy

Current incident-publication policy: Solo Staff combined review. One Staff member with both review and publication permission attests that the verification and legal/publication checklists were completed; this is recorded as one combined review — not represented as two independent signatures. The separate Site administrator cannot review records. Follow-up checks retain their separate independent approval and release rules: the performer cannot self-approve, and every check records its author, approver, and approval time. Staff perform the final publish action only after the system confirms the applicable approval. Where an officer is to be named, a qualified lawyer must perform the legal review or the combined solo review. Consent and publication basis are reviewed separately before publishing.

Corrections and retraction

If a published record is challenged and we cannot stand behind its stated verification status or institutional description, we correct the status or withdraw the record, promptly and visibly. A retraction is not a failure; publishing something we cannot support would be. Retracted records remain discoverable as retracted tombstones, never silently deleted, and every correction is logged on the record with its date. If you believe a record is inaccurate, contact us and we will review it.

What this site does not do

Contacting Copwatch India does not notify authorities, begin a legal case, provide emergency assistance, verify an account, or guarantee publication. This is not an emergency or legal-assistance service. We do not obstruct lawful police work, and we do not support revenge, doxxing, or mob action. Accountability serves honest officers as much as it serves the public: where abuse goes unrecorded, suspicion falls on everyone in uniform. Nothing on this site is legal advice. For advice on a specific case, consult a lawyer or your State Legal Services Authority.

Reading the aggregates

Counts show recorded allegations regardless of their individual evidence position; they are not counts of proven misconduct. Percentages are withheld until at least 20 records are published, and these records are not a representative sample. Coding carries noise where verifiers disagreed.

Limitations

Coverage is uneven by design: it reflects what the discovery index holds, what the queries returned, and what each agent extracted in a bounded session. Some states/UTs have zero accepted cases; absence is not evidence of absence. Outcome coding varies between verifiers. Judgment dates are day-precision except a few month-precision records. Judgments under two years old may still be under appeal; compensation figures are amounts ordered, not amounts shown paid or recovered. Districts are present-day districts. Summaries reproduce verified text with leftover identifying fragments redacted at build time; holdings give the verifier holding plus a verbatim court quote of at most 40 words.
