Economic freedom

Jailed for trading freely.

In the world’s freest economies — Switzerland, Singapore, the UAE, Hong Kong, El Salvador — buying bitcoin, holding dollars, vaulting gold and moving your own money are ordinary life. In India the same acts get people raided, charged and convicted. This register will document those convictions — each one attributed to the court that decided it.

What was criminalised?

Cryptocurrencies

Buying, holding and trading crypto

PMLA and enforcement cases against bitcoin and crypto users — and the banking-ban era that cut the whole industry off first.

Legal there: legal tender in El Salvador; licensed exchanges in Switzerland, Singapore and the UAE.

See these records → Foreign currency

Holding and spending forex

FEMA cases for keeping dollars, paying abroad, or transacting outside the authorised-dealer cage.

Legal there: hold and spend any currency freely in Panama, Hong Kong and Singapore.

See these records → Gold

Keeping and bringing in gold

Customs and “smuggling” convictions for carrying and storing the metal Indian families trust most.

Legal there: buy, vault and carry gold freely in Switzerland, Dubai and Singapore.

See these records → Moving money across borders

Sending your own money out

Hawala and FEMA cases for moving funds across borders outside the official channels and caps.

Legal there: free capital movement across most free economies — no permission slip needed.

See these records → Trading without permission

Selling where the state said no

Street vendors cleared, mandi and APMC cases against farmers selling outside the official markets.

Legal there: hawker markets in Hong Kong and Singapore license the trade instead of banning it.

See these records → Prices and stocks

Charging and storing too much

Essential Commodities Act “hoarding” and stock-limit cases for storing and pricing goods the market would clear.

Legal there: charge what the market bears — no stock police in free economies.

See these records → Other licensed livelihoods

Working without the state’s paper

Prosecutions for running a business, pantry, taxi or trade the licence raj never got around to permitting.

Legal there: start a business in a day in New Zealand and Singapore — by right, not by favour.

Methodology

What qualifies, and how we verify

The standard

A record qualifies when a court has convicted or adversely found against a person for a peaceful economic act — using crypto, holding forex or gold, moving money, trading, stocking or pricing — that is legal in the world’s freest economies. Full methodology, verification levels and the naming rule will be published here before the first record goes live; the standard follows the Babuwatch method: record, attribute, correct — and withhold names unless a naming gate clears them. Economic Freedom records court findings in the public interest. Each finding is attributed to the court that made it; inclusion establishes nothing beyond that finding, and convictions may be under appeal. Names are withheld unless a naming gate clears them, and records are corrected or withdrawn as facts develop.
