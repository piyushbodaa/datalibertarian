Economic freedom

Penalised for trading freely.

The Heritage Foundation’s 2026 Index of Economic Freedom ranks India the 132nd-freest economy (52.5, “mostly unfree”); Singapore leads at 84.4. This register documents the human side of that gap: Indians raided, penalised, charged and sometimes convicted for peaceful economic acts — crypto, dollars, gold, cross-border trade, unlicensed work. The comparisons with other economies below are this register’s argument, not legal findings; rules abroad differ in detail. Each action is attributed to the court or agency that took it.

The benchmark

Why these groups

Heritage 2026

The groups below follow the Heritage Foundation’s Index of Economic Freedom: 12 freedoms in 4 pillars — rule of law, government size, regulatory efficiency, open markets. India scores 52.5, 132nd of the ranked economies, “mostly unfree” — and scores lowest in the areas where these cases arise: financial freedom 40.0, investment freedom 40.0, property rights 48.3. Seven freedoms, nine groups: the ones where these enforcement actions arise. Government size is left out — the state doesn’t jail you for its own budget.

What was criminalised?

Financial freedom · Open markets

Buying, holding and trading crypto

PMLA and enforcement cases against bitcoin and crypto users — and the banking-ban era that cut the whole industry off first.

Legal there: legal tender in El Salvador; licensed exchanges in Singapore (#1), Switzerland (#2) and the UAE.

See these records → Financial freedom · Open markets

Holding and spending foreign currency

FEMA cases for keeping dollars, paying abroad, or transacting outside the authorised-dealer cage.

Legal there: hold and spend any currency freely in Panama, Hong Kong and Singapore (#1).

See these records → Financial freedom · Open markets

Keeping and bringing in gold

Customs and “smuggling” convictions for carrying and storing the metal Indian families trust most.

Compare: Switzerland (#2), Dubai and Singapore (#1) levy little or no duty on investment gold, though imports still carry declaration and, above set limits, permit rules.

See these records → Trade freedom · Open markets

Trading across borders

“Smuggling” convictions for moving goods — gold, electronics, everyday cargo — past one of the world’s highest tariff walls.

Legal there: free ports in Singapore (#1) and Hong Kong; Taiwan (#5) trades openly.

See these records → Investment freedom · Open markets

Moving and investing your capital

Hawala and FEMA cases for sending money out; prosecutions for putting capital where FDI caps said it may not go.

Legal there: open capital accounts in Luxembourg (#6), Ireland (#3) and Denmark (#7).

See these records → Business freedom · Regulatory efficiency

Opening and running a business

Licence-permit prosecutions: street vendors cleared, farmers booked for selling outside APMC mandis, trades shut for missing paper.

Legal there: start a business in a day in New Zealand (#12) and Singapore (#1).

See these records → Labor freedom · Regulatory efficiency

Hiring and firing

Contract-labour, ESI/PF and Industrial Disputes prosecutions for staffing decisions that employers elsewhere make freely.

Compare: Switzerland (#2), Singapore (#1) and Denmark (#7) regulate hiring and firing more lightly — though they too make employers pay statutory contributions (Singapore’s CPF, for one).

See these records → Monetary freedom · Regulatory efficiency

Charging and storing what you like

Essential Commodities Act “hoarding” and stock-limit cases for storing and pricing goods the market would clear.

Legal there: no price police in any top-ten economy.

See these records → Property rights · Rule of law

Owning, converting and building

Land-use conversion, building-plan and tenancy prosecutions — punished over the use of land you supposedly own.

Legal there: secure title and clear conversion rules in Australia (#4) and New Zealand (#12).

Methodology

What qualifies, and how we verify

The standard

A record qualifies when the state has prosecuted, penalised, detained, seized from or convicted a person for a peaceful economic act in one of the nine groups above — the kind of activity the top-ranked Heritage economies largely allow, although their exact rules differ. Cases that also allege cheating of private persons, violence or theft of public goods are left out. Each record says what the court or authority actually did and presumes nothing beyond it.

Sources and verification

Every record links its sources. V2 means an official record — a court judgment or order, a gazette, or a government or agency release — has been read and the record checked against it; quotes are copied verbatim. Only V2 records are published. A record that rests on news reports alone (V1) is held back until an official record is found. Each record also says what it could not confirm.

Names, coverage and corrections

Names of private individuals are withheld unless a naming gate clears them; officials acting in office and judges are named. Coverage is being built state by state, starting with Andhra Pradesh and the most recent cases, one record per category first. A record is a documented instance, not an estimate of how often this happens. To correct a record, write to contact@copwatchindia.org with the official document. The method follows Babuwatch: record, attribute, correct.
