What “FoS” means here

“Freedom of Speech” is the project's editorial lens — the reason these convictions are collected — not a claim that a court held any particular expression constitutionally protected. This section is a factual register of convictions involving speech or expression. Whether a conviction was consistent with Article 19(1)(a) is a legal question the record leaves to the judgment itself.

Why an empty page

No candidate has yet been verified to the publication standard, so the count is 0. Arrests, FIRs, pending prosecutions, censorship orders, website blocks, publication bans, civil damages and other restrictions without a qualifying conviction are excluded from these counts — they may be held internally as leads.

What we will never do

Describe violence, threats, fraud, stalking or coercion as peaceful expression. Where a conviction mixes expression with other conduct, the mixed context is preserved. Where the relationship to expression is ambiguous, the record is held for review rather than published. Unknowns stay unknown.

Inclusion policy

The shared evidence gate requires: a preserved source, an authenticated court, an explicit conviction for the defendant, a case reference, a V2/V3 verification decision, and an established connection between the particular conviction and the particular expression. The geographic scope is Indian jurisdiction; unknown citizenship is not labelled.

Corrections

If a record here is wrong, write to the project and it will be corrected or removed. Retracted records are deleted, not quietly kept.

These registers list only people a court convicted — not anyone merely accused, charge-sheeted, suspended or acquitted.
