Civil liberties

Freedom is the point.

Indians are punished — arrested, charged, convicted — for exercising the freedoms the Constitution promises: speaking, believing, worshipping, assembling. This register documents those cases of state overreach, starting with freedom of speech and freedom of religion, with more sections to be added.

Methodology

What qualifies, and how we verify

The standard

A record qualifies when the state has arrested, charged, detained, penalised or convicted a person for exercising a basic freedom — speaking, writing, posting, believing or worshipping — or has stripped a legal status or protection because of it. An arrest is recorded as an arrest; the court’s findings, where there are any, are reported as the court’s.

Sources and verification

Every record links its sources. V2 means an official record — a court judgment or order, a gazette, or a government or agency release — has been read and the record checked against it; quotes are copied verbatim. V1 means the facts rest on two or more independent news reports and no official record has been found yet. Each record also says what it could not confirm.

Names, coverage and corrections

Names of private individuals are withheld unless a naming gate clears them; officials acting in office and judges are named. Coverage is being built state by state, starting with Andhra Pradesh and the most recent cases, one record per category first. A record is a documented instance, not an estimate of how often this happens. To correct a record, write to contact@copwatchindia.org with the official document. The method follows Babuwatch: record, attribute, correct.
